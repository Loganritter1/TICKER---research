## Question

What are five years of Tesla's statements worth, built from assumptions I can defend?

Tesla, Inc. (NASDAQ: TSLA) is modeled in [tesla_proforma.py](tesla_proforma.py). The base case values the established automotive, energy, and services businesses only; it assigns no separate Robotaxi or Optimus revenue.

## Company-specific line

Tesla's energy generation and storage business is the line that makes this model different. It grew 27% in 2025 and earned a 29.8% segment gross margin, versus a 10% decline in automotive revenue and a 17.8% automotive gross margin. The model therefore projects energy revenue separately, while keeping automotive flat in 2026 before a gradual recovery. Robotaxi and Optimus are excluded rather than treated as present operating revenue.

## Labelled assumption set

| Assumption | Value | Label | Reason |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Automotive revenue growth | 0%, 4%, 5%, 5%, 4% | Judgment | 2025 automotive revenue declined 10%; the base case assumes no immediate rebound, then a modest recovery rather than a return to earlier growth. |
| Energy revenue growth | 20%, 18%, 15%, 12%, 10% | Judgment | 2025 growth was 27% with strong deployments; I step it down as the base gets larger. |
| Services revenue growth | 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, 4% | Judgment | The installed fleet should support service revenue, but I do not assume it grows faster than energy indefinitely. |
| Consolidated gross margin | 18.0% to 20.0% | Judgment | 2025 was 18.0%; higher-energy mix can help, but pricing and tariffs make a rapid margin recovery hard to defend. |
| SG&A ÷ gross profit | 34.0% to 30.0% | Judgment | 2025 was 34.1%; modest scale efficiency is assumed, not a return to 2023's level. |
| R&D ÷ revenue | 7.0% to 5.5% | Judgment | Tesla is investing in AI and autonomy, so the model starts above 2025's 6.8% and only gradually declines. |
| Stock-based compensation ÷ revenue | 3.0% | History | FY2025 cash flow reports $2.825bn of non-cash stock-based compensation ÷ FY2025 revenue; it is added back in FCFE and credited to equity. |
| Depreciation ÷ opening PP&E | 12.4% | History | $5.03bn FY2025 depreciation ÷ $40.64bn FY2025 PP&E (the code applies this to opening PP&E). |
| Capital spending | 20,000; 16,000; 14,000; 13,000; 12,000 | Guidance / judgment | Tesla said 2026 capital expenditures are expected to exceed $20bn. Later years step down, but remain above recent history because AI and manufacturing investment is ongoing. |
| Inventory days | 58.2 | History | FY2025 inventory ÷ FY2025 cost of revenue × 365. |
| Other assets and liabilities | 42.9% and 49.1% of revenue | History | FY2025 aggregate reported non-modeled assets (excluding separately modeled short-term investments) and liabilities ÷ revenue; keeps the simplified balance sheet linked to sales. |
| Tax rate | 27.0% | History | FY2025 provision ÷ pre-tax income. |
| Debt repayment | 500 annually | Judgment | Tesla has net cash and repaid debt in 2025; a small recurring repayment is more cautious than assuming new debt-funded expansion. |
| Minimum cash / revolver | 4,000 / 0 | Judgment | Tesla had $16.5bn of cash and $27.5bn of short-term investments at FY2025, so a $4bn cash floor is conservative within a model that aggregates investments into other assets. No undisclosed revolver is assumed. |
| Cost of equity / terminal growth | 10.0% / 2.5% | Judgment | A conservative required return and long-run nominal growth rate; the terminal value is highly sensitive to both. |
| Diluted shares | 3,528 | History | FY2025 diluted weighted-average shares from the 2025 10-K. |
| Robotaxi / Optimus revenue | 0 | Judgment | No separate reported revenue or durable unit economics were disclosed in the filing, so the base case does not monetize optionality. |

The judgment I would defend longest is zero Robotaxi and Optimus revenue in the base case: it avoids converting an aspiration into cash flow before Tesla reports durable commercial economics. I would change it only after Tesla discloses recurring revenue, operating costs, and evidence of scalable unit economics.

The surprising filing number was the more-than-$20 billion 2026 capital-expenditure expectation: it is much larger than the $8.5 billion spent in 2025 and dominates near-term cash flow.

## Build, checks, and valuation

Run `python tesla_proforma.py`. Cash is calculated from FCFE, not typed. The balance-sheet check prints zero for every forecast year; an assertion stops the model if a balance-sheet gap exceeds $0.05 million or cash falls below its floor. FY2026 sells part of the separately modeled FY2025 $27.5bn short-term-investment balance to maintain the $4bn cash floor; this is an asset reclassification, not invented operating cash or debt. No year draws a revolver.

The base case has negative FCFE in 2026–2029 because the guided AI/manufacturing capital-spending step-up exceeds operating cash generation. A terminal value on a negative cash flow is not a number because the perpetuity-growth formula assumes a positive, sustainable cash flow. The model therefore assigns no value to negative annual FCFE and values terminal cash flow only after it turns positive in 2030.

**Model result:** the model produces a $9,246.7 million equity value and **$2.62 per diluted share**. FY2026–FY2029 FCFE is negative and receives no standalone value; 93.2% of the resulting value is the discounted 2030 terminal value, which makes the result especially sensitive to the recovery and terminal assumptions.

### Broken-balance test

I temporarily changed the opening cash balance from $16,513 million to $0. The model refused to run:

```text
AssertionError: FY2026E balance-sheet gap: -16513.0
```

I restored the reported opening cash balance afterward. This confirms that the model does not value a broken balance sheet.
